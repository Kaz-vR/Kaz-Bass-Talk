RUIN-1 — WHAT THE SYNTH CAN AND CAN'T DO
=========================================
A short overview so you can judge whether a sound is possible on RUIN-1
before suggesting it. RUIN-1 is a dark, gritty, tape-worn subtractive synth
(made for LORN-style sounds), not an all-purpose workstation.


WHAT IT CAN DO
--------------

Voices
- Mono (with legato and glide/portamento) or poly up to 8 voices.
- Velocity sensitivity, pitch bend (±2 semitones), mod wheel.
- "Analog" drift: each oscillator and unison voice drifts slightly in pitch,
  so sounds feel loose and alive instead of digital.

Two main oscillators (A and B)
- Waves: sine, triangle, saw, pulse, PWM-saw (the rave "hoover"), a plucked
  string model, and one built-in wavetable that morphs sine → triangle → saw
  → square → vocal.
- Shape knob: pulse width on pulse/PWM-saw, morph position on the wavetable.
- Octave, semitone and fine tuning (detuning A against B for beating, fifths,
  octaves, any interval).
- Unison up to 7 voices per oscillator, with detune and stereo spread
  (supersaw-style thickness).
- Hard sync of B to A (sync sweeps, "chainsaw" leads).
- Each oscillator has its own low cut (or low-pass / band) before the rest.

Sub oscillator
- A clean extra layer under the note: sine, warm sine, triangle, square,
  pulse or saw, -4 to +4 octaves from the note.
- By default it bypasses all dirt and effects, so the low end stays clean.
- Its own soft clipper for density.

Noise layer (one type at a time)
- Long/continuous textures: vinyl crackle, dust, static, tape hiss, air
  (breath), rumble, white noise.
- "Hit" versions of each: one burst per note (attack transients, breath puffs
  on brass/flutes, noise snaps), with adjustable tail length.
- Adjustable tone (dark ↔ bright), density, tail (how long it lingers after
  the note) and its own low cut.
- Octave/speed control: slow it down for dark thumps and roars, speed it up
  for small ticks.
- KEY mode: the noise becomes playable across the keyboard like a sample,
  with glide (tape-stop effects, a noise "kit" across the keys).
- Can go through the main filter, or straight to the output untouched.

Filter and dirt
- Main filter types: 24 dB low-pass, band-pass, 303-style diode with accent
  (acid squelch), notch, comb (metallic/plucked ringing), low-pass gate
  (Buchla-style pluck), 24 dB high-pass.
- Cutoff, resonance, key tracking, and a dedicated filter envelope.
- Drive before the filter (warm saturation the filter then shapes).
- Distortion after the filter: soft, hard, fold (wavefolder), tube, fuzz;
  with a "clean below" split so the low end stays clean.
- Bitcrusher (bit depth and sample-rate reduction).
- Tape: saturation, wow & flutter (warped pitch), age (dull, worn top).

RUIN (signature distortion chain)
- Up to 4 stages of moving filters (notch, peak, band, comb, phaser), each
  into its own distortion: neuro/industrial basses, growls, metallic grind.
- The stages can move with each note or wander randomly; plus saturation and
  an OTT-style compressor in the chain.
- Can be applied to the oscillators while leaving the noise untouched.

Envelopes
- Amp envelope (ADSR): the volume shape of every note. Each segment has an
  adjustable curve (snappy analog or a slow swell): anything from plucks and
  stabs to long swells and pads.
- Filter envelope (its own ADSR, also with curves, switchable on/off): sweeps
  the main filter's cutoff on every note. Its AMOUNT (the "ENV" setting next
  to Cutoff) sets how far and which way it sweeps, in octaves:
    - up to about ±10 octaves, i.e. from the lowest cutoff all the way to the
      top: a tiny amount is a subtle bite, a large one a full "wow" sweep;
    - positive opens the filter at the start of the note and closes back
      down as it decays (plucks, stabs, acid squelch, brassy "bwah" attacks,
      the classic bass "wow");
    - negative does the opposite: the note starts darker and the filter dips
      (reverse/swelling sweeps).
  With a short decay and low sustain this is the plucky "pew"; with a slow
  attack it's a slow opening swell; with high sustain the filter opens and
  stays open while the note is held. The filter envelope can also be used as
  a modulation source for other targets.

Movement (6 LFOs + a 12-slot modulation matrix)
- LFO shapes: sine, triangle, saw up/down, square, sample & hold, smooth
  random (wander), drift and slow drift (organic, never-repeating motion).
- Per-note shapes: bloom (a slow rise when you hold a note), drop (a fall:
  pitch drops, plucks, tape stops), random (a new value per note).
- Free rate or synced to tempo, fade-in, retrigger.
- Modulation sources: the 6 LFOs, both envelopes, velocity, mod wheel, key.
- Modulation targets: pitch (whole note, both oscillators, A or B alone, sub,
  noise), shape (A, B or both), unison detune, levels (overall, A, B, sub,
  noise), filter cutoff, resonance and drive, distortion drive/mix/tone,
  vibrato.
- Mod wheel "moves": one wheel gesture that changes several things together
  (pressure, snarl, open, choke, vibrato, unstable, decay, bloom).

Effects (after the voices)
- Chorus (classic, ensemble, hyper/supersaw-wide), flanger, phaser,
  OTT-style multiband compression, 4-band EQ.
- Each effect can leave the low end untouched.
- Always-on safety: mono low end and an output limiter.

Typical sounds it does well
- Dark, saturated basses: Reese, sub basses, distorted/industrial basses,
  neuro growls, acid (303) lines, 808-style bass with a pitch drop.
- Gritty or wide leads, sync leads, hoovers, PWM and supersaw-ish leads.
- Dark pads that slowly bloom and drift, warm analog pads, string-ish and
  brass-ish synth pads.
- Plucks, keys-like and mallet-ish synth tones, arps.
- Noise sounds: crackle and dust beds, breath, rumbles, noise hits, tape-stop
  and playable noise effects.
- Anything "worn": tape wobble, crushed, lo-fi, vintage-unstable.


WHAT IT CAN'T DO
----------------

Sources
- Only 2 oscillators + 1 sub + 1 noise layer. No third oscillator.
- No samples or sample import, no wavetable import (only the one built-in
  morph table), no user-drawn waveforms.
- No FM, ring modulation or audio-rate cross-modulation on the main pages
  (only hard sync).
- No additive or granular synthesis, no physical models besides the simple
  plucked string.
- One noise type at a time; the noise layer is shared by all voices.
- Realistic acoustic instruments (piano, guitar, real strings, real brass,
  drums, vocals/choirs, bells) are out of reach; only synth versions of them.

Filters
- One main filter per voice (no dual, serial or parallel filters).
- No formant/vowel filter (removed): no talking or vocal "yoi" basses.

Modulation
- Only 2 envelopes (amp and filter); extra rises and falls come from the
  bloom/drop LFO shapes.
- 6 LFOs and 12 modulation slots; no step sequencer, no drawable LFO shapes,
  no LFO-on-LFO modulation.
- Effects, tape and LFO speeds cannot be modulated.
- No aftertouch (the plugin version has MPE).

Time and rhythm
- No arpeggiator, no sequencer, no rhythmic gate/volume shaper or
  tempo-wobble module: rhythmic chopping, gating and dubstep-style wobbles
  are meant to be done after the synth (e.g. ShaperBox). Slow, tempo-synced
  LFO movement is possible.
- No reverb and no delay: space is added after the synth.

Dynamics and output
- No compressor except the OTT-style ones; the output limiter and the mono
  low end can't be switched off.
- No per-oscillator panning; width comes from unison, chorus and effects.
- Max 8 voices; in chords only the lowest note plays the sub.
